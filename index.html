Dual-flywheel launcher

IB Physics Extended Essay, 2025 to 2026

I wanted to measure how spin changes a lacrosse ball’s flight, so I designed and built a launcher from FRC parts. A laser-cut plywood frame holds it at 45 degrees, and two NEO Vortex motors each drive a stack of rubber wheels, one above the ball and one below. The speed difference between the wheels sets the spin and their average speed sets the launch speed, so I could change one without changing the other.

I launched the ball five times at each of nine settings, from the top wheel spinning 2000 RPM faster than the bottom (topspin) to the reverse (backspin). The average landing distance rose from 2.215 m to 2.451 m, against 2.334 m with no spin, as the Magnus effect predicts. The shift was about six times larger than my prediction from published baseball data. Our competition robot’s shooter also uses two flywheels.

Shoot-on-the-move

FRC Team 5829, 2026 season, written in Java

Our robot has no turret, so the whole chassis has to aim, and stopping to line up every shot costs time. The ball keeps the robot’s velocity after it leaves the shooter, so my code aims at a shifted point instead of the goal, using flight times I measured on the field, and recalculates until the aim settles.

In testing it scored on more than 90% of shots taken while moving. I also tuned the PID and feedforward control for the shooter’s flywheels.

Research

Semantic Chapters for Traceable Question Answering over Long Interview Transcripts

Poster at the 2026 AISES National Conference, Portland, Oregon, October 15 to 17. Selected through peer review. Advised by Dr. Qi Yu, School of AI, Tongji University.

Our podcast’s AI answers questions from hour-long interviews, so every answer needs to point back to what a guest actually said. I split each transcript into topic chapters and search within them, then compared that with keyword search (BM25) and fixed-size chunks on answer accuracy, recall, citation correctness, faithfulness and response time.

Chapter-based retrieval improved correct answers by more than 15% over fixed-size chunks.
